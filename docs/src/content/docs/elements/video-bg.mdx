Background video goes wrong in one way everyone has seen: a black rectangle, or a frozen
frame with a spinner, before the picture arrives. medkit shows the video's first frame as a
poster, and hands over to the video only once a real frame has painted, so the switch cannot
be seen. With no JavaScript, the poster and your overlay are the page.

rendered.

## Rendering it

`renderVideoBg(clips, options)`, or the React and Astro `VideoBg` components, which write the
same bytes. Each clip is `{ video, title, overlay?, captions? }`: the video's manifest, which
holds its poster; a title, announced when a visitor switches clips; HTML shown over the clip
(a React node with React); and caption tracks for the popup that expand opens.

| Option | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `controls` | `pause`, `sound`, `expand`, `nav`, space-separated | `pause` |
| `autoadvance` | Start the next clip when one ends, instead of looping | off |
| `priority` | The first poster loads at high priority, for a hero | off |
| `intro` | A colour instead of the first poster; see [intro colour](../../guides/intro/) | none |
| `labels` | Replacement labels; see [translations](../../guides/translations/) | English |
| `baseUrl`, `sizes` | Where the files are served; how wide the poster displays | `/media/`, `100vw` |

## When it plays

1. Nothing but the poster loads until the element is within 200 px of the viewport.
2. With reduced motion, Save-Data or a 2G connection, the poster stays and no video loads.
3. It picks the smallest rendition that covers its width × device pixel ratio, up to 1080p,
   AV1 where encoded and supported. It steps down for a slow connection, measured from
   `navigator.connection` where it exists, and from how fast the poster arrived.
4. It starts once the browser can play through, or 3 s are buffered ahead, or after 5 s
   whatever happens. Until then the poster, which is the first frame, stays.
5. It pauses when less than 10% is on screen or the tab is hidden, and resumes when both come
   back, unless the visitor paused it. A visitor's pause is remembered for the session.
6. A stall mid-play keeps the last frame, with no spinner, and resumes on its own.

If autoplay is refused, as in iOS Low Power Mode, the poster stays and the pause button
offers play.

## Controls

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `pause` | Pause and play. Always shown, unless every clip is under 5 seconds (WCAG 2.2.2) |
| `sound` | Turn sound on or off; shown only for a clip that kept its audio |
| `expand` | Opens the clip in the [player's popup](../../guides/expand/), with sound, at the same moment |
| `nav` | Previous and next, with "2 / 4", which screen readers hear as "2 of 4" |

With two or more clips, each loops until the visitor presses Next, or with `autoadvance`
plays the next when it ends. At 75% through a clip, only the next clip's poster and first
seconds are fetched, and nothing with Save-Data. Clips crossfade over 400 ms, or cut with
reduced motion.

## Accessibility

- The video is decorative and hidden from assistive technology; the clip's title and overlay
  carry its meaning.
- Every control is a real `<button>` at least 44 × 44 px, whose label says what it will do
  ("Turn sound on").
- Inactive clips' overlays are `inert`. A manual switch is announced politely ("Crowd at
  night, 2 of 4"); an automatic one is not.
- Controls stay visible in Windows high contrast mode.
